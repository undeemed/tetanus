The `tools/post-execute` additional-contexts channel served (`crates/turn/src/events.rs`, `crates/turn/src/engine.rs`, TC-POSTCTX-1..6), closing the clause three separate parity rows pointed at. The event now answers a `PostToolDecision` - an outcome plus contexts - instead of a bare `ToolOutcome`. Two fields because a listener has two different things to say: the outcome is what the model reads as this call's result, the contexts are what it should read next. A guard that made its point by appending to the tool's output would be corrupting a tool author's contract, and a tool parsing its own result back would find a sentence nobody wrote - so the note travels beside the result and arrives as its own `user/message`. Contexts ride the decision rather than being appended by the listener, which is what lets the loop hold them to the commit: a call whose result is never committed, one behind an earlier call's fault, contributes no context either, and a listener writing straight to the journal could not be held to that. They commit after the result and are delivered at the next step boundary through the durable queue, so the complaint never precedes the thing complained about, and a note produced by the step that ended the turn stays queued for the next turn rather than being dropped - the boundary a guard cares about most is exactly where a lossy channel would lose it. A turn whose listeners attach nothing writes no queue record at all, so a deployment that does not use the seam pays nothing and its journals diff clean. The engine now owns its session's queue, folded at construction from the journal's own suffix: `own_suffix` skips a fork seed, because those splices are the parent's and were normalized against the parent's list, so folding them would hand the child a queue neither session ever had. A journal that will not fold fails the engine rather than being absorbed, since running with an empty queue would silently drop input a person is still waiting on; `crates/engine/src/convert.rs` splits the three causes the way the rest of that table does - a refused append reads as the journal failing, a splice that does not apply reads as the journal damaged and carries its seq, and a duplicate identity is this build's bug. Upstream folds contexts onto both an `accept` and a `block` decision; tetanus has one outcome, so there is no second variant and the blocked-call case has no counterpart. Four mutations, all caught.
