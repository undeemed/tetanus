The job store's first caller (`run_in_background` on the `shell` tool, plus `job_list`, `job_output` and `job_kill`, TC-PORT-JOB-13..18). The store landed with the workflow rescue and had no caller; the process lane had named this wiring and declined to build a second store for it, so the clause sat in two rows at once. A background command answers at once with a job id and its record is written before the work starts, because a job that began with no record is work nobody can find while a record whose work never started is one read away from saying so. A running job is an `ok` answer rather than a failure, since "ask again" and "try something else" are different instructions. The output is read back off the durable record rather than streamed and consumed, so two reads agree and a turn that died between them loses nothing. One defect was found by running the binary and could not have been found by the unit cases: the first cut registered the three job tools only when a store was composed, so `tetanus tools` advertised five exec tools where a run offers eight - the catalogue-versus-run disagreement contract section 4.7.3 forbids, invisible to every suite because each composition was internally consistent and the two were never compared. They are declared always now and explain themselves when there is nowhere to keep a record. `job_kill` stops the session's work through the turn's own interrupt, which reaches a process group rather than a handle; aiming at one job needs the group id on the record and is named in the gap.
